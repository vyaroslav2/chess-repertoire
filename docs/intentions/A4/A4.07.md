### A4.07 — Find or create the position-cache entry for this new board

**What the code does**  
The generator sends the position after White's move to the shared position-cache flow, [[PC.01]].

It passes:

- the FEN after White's move;
    
- no opening metadata;
    
- the extended move history from [[A4.06]].
    

In the code:

`await getOrCreatePositionCache(fenAfterWhite, undefined, newHistory);`

A4 does not use the returned `PositionCache` row directly here. The purpose of the call is to make sure that a shared cached record for this board exists.

Because the move history is supplied, PC can also try to find Wikibooks opening text when its own rules allow it.

ECO and opening name are not supplied at this point. The PC diagram notes that positions created after White's and Black's moves are normally created with history but without opening metadata. Those details may be added later if the same position itself reaches the human-data-fetching stage and Masters provides them.

After this call finishes, A4 continues to [[A4.32]].

**Why this matters**  
The position after White's move gets its own shared cache record even before the generator has chosen Black's reply.

That keeps general information about the chess position separate from the repertoire tree itself.

The move history also gives the position-cache flow enough context to look for opening text.

The detailed rules for creating, reusing and enriching the `PositionCache` row belong to the PC intentions rather than A4.07.

**Why it may have been designed this way**  
Likely: every board position encountered by the generator is meant to have a reusable shared cache entry, regardless of whether it is currently White's turn or Black's turn.

Keeping this work inside PC also prevents A4 from duplicating the FEN-normalisation and opening-text logic.
