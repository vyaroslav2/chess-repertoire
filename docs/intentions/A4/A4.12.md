### A4.12 — Record White's move as leading to this node, storing its popularity and the new cumulative probability

**What the code does**  
The generator records the current White move as a repertoire move from the current parent node to the node representing the position after White's move.

It stores:

- the White move's SAN;
    
- `playerTurn: "OPPONENT"`;
    
- `prob` — the local popularity of this White move from its parent position;
    
- `trueProbability` — the cumulative probability of this exact route after the White move.
    

So this move record connects:

the current repertoire position

-> White's move

-> the repertoire position after White's move.

The destination node may have just been created in [[A4.10]], or it may be an existing unexpanded node that [[A4.10]] reused.

After recording the move, the algorithm continues to [[A4.14]].

**Why this matters**  
This preserves the actual repertoire path.

The destination node represents the chess position after White's move, while this repertoire-move record explains how the current branch reached that position.

The two probability fields have different meanings:

- `prob` = how often this White move is played from the immediate parent position;
    
- `trueProbability` = how likely this exact complete route is from the repertoire root.
    

That distinction is important for transpositions. Several different repertoire moves can eventually lead to the same shared training position while keeping their own separate route probabilities.

Recording the move before choosing Black's reply also means the White side of the repertoire path already exists even if the later Black-response stage fails.

**Why it may have been designed this way**  
Known: the repertoire needs to preserve each distinct incoming route even when several routes may eventually share the same training position.

Keeping the move as a separate record also lets the project store local move probability and exact-route probability independently from the destination node's combined `cumulativeProb`.
