### A4.26 — Save the training card on the position after White's move

**What the code does**  
The generator creates a new empty FSRS card:

`const emptyCard = createEmptyCard();`

It then saves or updates a `RepertoirePositionStat` record for the position after White's move.

That record is identified by:

- the current repertoire;
    
- the repertoire node after White's move.
    

It points to the Black move created in [[A4.25]] through:

`targetMoveId: dbBlackMove.id`

So conceptually the card says:

> "When you see this position after White's move, the move you are supposed to recall is this Black response."

If no training-card record exists yet, the generator creates one with:

- the selected Black move as its target;
    
- the explanation text;
    
- a fresh FSRS review state;[^2]
    
- due date;
    
- stability;
    
- difficulty;
    
- elapsed days;
    
- scheduled days;
    
- repetitions;
    
- lapses;
    
- state;
    
- last-review value.
    

If the card already exists, the generator updates only:

- `targetMoveId`;
    
- `explanation`.
    

It does **not** reset the existing review schedule or review history.

The explanation text currently contains[^5]:

- the human score;
    
- the weighted human volume;
    
- the selected engine evaluation.
    

It does not include the evaluation-source label, even though that source was printed in [[A4.22]] and stored on the Black repertoire move in [[A4.25]].

After saving the training card, the algorithm continues to [[A4.31]].

**Why this matters**  
This is the point where a repertoire position becomes an actual SRS training item.

The card belongs to the **position after White's move**, because that is the position the user will be shown and must respond to.

The answer is not stored directly as SAN on the card. Instead, `targetMoveId` points to the Black `RepertoireMove`, which in turn knows the SAN and destination position.

Keeping the review state on the card separately from the move means the chess data and SRS scheduling data have different responsibilities.

The update behaviour is also important: if the target Black move changes later, the existing review history is preserved rather than starting the card from zero again.[^4]

**Why it may have been designed this way**  
Likely: the position itself is the SRS prompt, while the selected Black move is the answer.

Using an upsert also lets generation create missing cards while preserving the learning history of cards that already exist.

The decision to preserve the existing FSRS schedule when changing the target move is a separate product decision, because the learner may effectively be learning a different answer even though the position is unchanged.
