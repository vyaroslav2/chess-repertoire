### A2.17 — Has the trap/threat continuation depth reached 8?

**What the code does**  
The generator checks the `trapDepth` value carried by the current queued position.

The check is:

`trapDepth >= 8`

So:

- `0` to `7` -> no;
    
- `8` or more -> yes.
    

`trapDepth` starts at `0` for an ordinary branch.

When a trap or threat starts a continuation, the next queued position receives a `trapDepth` of `1`.

After that, while the continuation is active, each later full move increases the counter by `1`.

Reaching `8` therefore does not mean that eight traps or threats have occurred. It means that the continuation counter itself has reached eight full moves.

A trap inside an existing trap continuation: see [[A4.28]].

If the answer is yes, the algorithm goes to [[A2.18]] and stops the branch.

If the answer is no, the position continues to [[A2.16]], which hands it to A3.

**Why this matters**  
This counter gives a trap or threat line a limited amount of continuation after the special line has begun.

Its purpose is to let the line continue far enough to show the response or refutation rather than ending immediately because the move was rare.

The current code reaches this check only after the ordinary depth-limit check in [[A2.10]]. See [[A2.10]] for the related `#bug`.

The rules that decide whether a White move is an Amateur Trap or a Master Threat are separate from this box and belong to [[A3.05]].

**Why it may have been designed this way**  
Known: the value `8` was chosen when the continuation was mistakenly being thought of in plies rather than full moves. It is otherwise an arbitrary allowance intended to give a trap or threat enough room for its refutation to be played out.[^2]

Counting later full moves gives the continuation a finite maximum length once it has begun.
