### A1.16 — Take the item at the front

**What the code does**
The next item is taken off the **front** of the queue, and the queue shrinks by one. New positions are always added to the back, in [[A4.27]].

Taking from the front while adding to the back means positions are dealt with in the order they were discovered. Everything one move deep is finished before anything two moves deep begins.

**Why this matters**
This single choice shapes the entire tree, and it is the reason your repertoire looks the way it does.

Because the shallowest positions are always taken first, the tree fills out level by level. Stop a run at any point and what you have is complete as far as it goes — every line covered to the same depth, with nothing half-explored.

Taking from the back instead would reverse that. The run would follow one line as deep as the cap allows, then back up and follow the next. Stop it early and you would have a few very deep lines and whole openings untouched.

For a repertoire, level by level is clearly right. You want every reply White might play at move two before you want move five of one line. It also means the depth cap behaves predictably: raise it by one and every branch grows by one, rather than some branches growing and others not.

It also affects what a partial run is worth. Since runs cannot currently be resumed, and a second run crashes, any interrupted run is thrown away — but the work it did was at least evenly spread, so nothing was wasted on one deep line at the expense of the rest.[^1]

**Why it may have been designed this way**
Known: the generator's own opening message calls itself a breadth-first tree generator, so working level by level is deliberate and named as such.

**Also affects:** [[A1.14]] (which asks whether anything is left), [[A4.27]] (which adds to the back), [[R.11]] (which seeds the queue), [[A1]] (this is the decision that shapes the whole diagram)

Notes:

[^1]: #note Taking from the front, adding to the back, is the decision behind the shape of the whole tree: complete and shallow rather than narrow and deep. Recorded here because it is easy to change by accident — a single word — and the consequence would not be obvious until a run had finished.
