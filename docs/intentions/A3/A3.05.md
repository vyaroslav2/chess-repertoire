### A3.05 — For each move in the pool, run the filter (see rules panel). Keep the ones marked "include", along with their reason, threat/trap flags, and popularity

**What the code does**  
The generator runs every distinct White move from [[A3.04]] through the same filter.

For each SAN move, the filter separately looks up that move in:

- Masters;
    
- Elite;
    
- Amateur.
    

So the fact that [[A3.04]] keeps only one copy of each SAN does not lose the separate statistics belonging to that move in the different player groups.

The filter first works out several values and flags.

**1. Popularity**

Popularity is:

`this move's amateur games / total amateur games at this position`

If there are no amateur games for the position, popularity is `0`.

**2. Master Threat flag[^4]**

The move is flagged as a Master Threat when:

- it has at least `15` Masters games;
    
- its smoothed Masters White score is at least `58%`.
    

The smoothing uses the shared smoothing calculation with a prior of 50 games at 52%.

**3. Master Threat popularity floor**

If the Master Threat flag is set and the move's amateur popularity is below `1%`, the filter changes its working popularity to exactly `1%`.

This does not mean the move actually occurred in 1% of amateur games. It is an artificial floor used by the algorithm for that flagged move.

**4. Amateur Trap flag[^3]**

The move is flagged as an Amateur Trap when:

- it has at least `15` amateur games;
    
- its smoothed amateur White score is at least `58%`;
    
- it is not already a Master Threat.
    

So this particular Amateur Trap flag and the Master Threat flag are mutually exclusive at the point where the flags are first worked out.

**5. Mainline popularity bar**

The ordinary popularity requirement depends on the current full-move number:

- moves `1-4`: at least `5%`;[^1]
    
- moves `5-8`: at least `10%`;
    
- move `9` onwards: at least `15%`.
    

The filter then decides whether to include the move. The first matching rule wins.

**Rule 1 - Mainline**

If the move's working popularity is at or above the current bar:

- include it;
    
- reason: `"Mainline"`.[^2]
    

Because the threat/trap flags were calculated before this decision, a move can have reason `"Mainline"` while still carrying a Master Threat or Amateur Trap flag. The flags are not cleared when the move qualifies as a Mainline.

**Rule 2 - Master Threat**

Otherwise, if the Master Threat flag is set:

- include it;
    
- reason: `"Master Threat"`.
    

**Rule 3 - Amateur Trap**

Otherwise, include the move as an Amateur Trap if either:

- its Amateur Trap flag is already set;
    
- or its popularity is at least `1%` and its raw amateur White win rate is at least `55%`.
    

In the second case, the code also sets the Amateur Trap flag even though the move did not satisfy the earlier smoothed `58%` trap test.

The reason is `"Amateur Trap"`.

**Rule 4 - Drop**

If none of those rules match, the move is not included.

There is also an overriding condition: if the position has no amateur games at all, the inclusion rules are never entered. Nothing is included, including a move that otherwise satisfies the Master Threat test.

The Elite move is looked up for each SAN, but its result is not used by any of these White-move filter calculations or inclusion rules. Elite data therefore cannot make a White move pass this filter.

For every move that survives, A3 keeps:

- its SAN;
    
- its inclusion reason;
    
- its Master Threat and Amateur Trap flags;
    
- its working popularity;
    
- its Masters statistics;
    
- its amateur statistics.
    

The surviving moves then continue to [[A3.06]].

**Why this matters**  
This box determines which of the opponent's White moves the repertoire will actually cover.

The mainline rule covers moves because they are sufficiently common.

The Master Threat and Amateur Trap rules provide exceptions for moves that may be too uncommon for the ordinary popularity bar but are considered dangerous enough to deserve coverage.

The order matters because the first matching reason wins. A move that is both common enough for Mainline and already carries a threat/trap flag receives reason `"Mainline"` rather than `"Master Threat"` or `"Amateur Trap"`.

However, the flag remains attached. Later code uses those flags independently of the reason. In A4, a Mainline-reason move carrying an Amateur Trap or Master Threat flag is treated as a trap or threat rather than as an ordinary mainline.

The popularity floor for Master Threats also matters beyond this filter because the adjusted `1%` value is the probability passed onwards with that move rather than its original lower amateur frequency.

**Why it may have been designed this way**  
Likely: the ordinary popularity bars are intended to keep the repertoire focused on moves that opponents are reasonably likely to play, while the threat/trap rules provide exceptions for rarer moves that are unusually successful for White.

Likely: smoothing and the minimum-game requirements are intended to reduce the chance that very small samples with extreme win rates are classified as threats or traps.

The exact intentions behind Master Threat and Amateur Trap classification need to be settled separately below.
