### A3.04 — Build the pool: every distinct move that appears in ANY of the three sets. A move in only one list still goes in the pool

**What the code does**  
The generator builds one combined pool of White moves from the three human datasets returned by F:

- Masters;
    
- Elite;
    
- Amateur.
    

It adds every move found in any of those datasets.

The pool is based on the move's [[SAN]], such as `e4`, `Nf3` or `O-O`.

If the same SAN move appears in more than one dataset, it still appears only once in the combined pool. This does not discard the other datasets' statistics. The pool keeps only one copy of the move name, and [[A3.05]] separately looks up that move in Masters, Elite and Amateur when it evaluates it.

If a move appears in only one of the three datasets, it still goes into the pool.

At this point, being in the pool does not mean that the move will be included in the repertoire. The pool is only the complete set of distinct White moves that will be tested by the filter in [[A3.05]].

**Why this matters**  
This makes sure the filter gets a chance to examine every White move found in any of the three human datasets.

The generator does not require a move to appear in Masters, Elite and Amateur before considering it.

It also avoids testing the same chess move several times just because it appears in more than one dataset.

The three datasets are combined only to decide which distinct moves need to be examined. Their individual statistics remain separate and are used by [[A3.05]] when each move is filtered.

**Why it may have been designed this way**  
Likely: using the union of all three datasets prevents a potentially interesting move from being missed simply because it appears in only one player group.

Removing duplicates by SAN also gives the filter one clear pass over each distinct legal move rather than processing the same move separately for Masters, Elite and Amateur.
